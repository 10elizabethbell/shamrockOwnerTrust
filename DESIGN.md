# Design System: Shamrock Transportation

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Night Drive Down I-95"**

The whole page is one drive down I-95. It opens at the roadside at dusk: a green interstate guide sign stands on two steel posts above the road, under a sky fading up from night blue, and interstate green is also shamrock green, so the mark and the road share one colour. The sign appears once. Ed introduces himself plainly on aluminum, with his cell number in sign green. The light "sign-back aluminum" run reads like the back of a road sign: cool, matte, plain. The close is the highway after dark: blue-green, never black, with the car driving it. Headlight amber is the only warm light on the page. At rest it belongs to calling and to the road's centre line, and nothing else in the interface; the dusk glow on the hero's horizon is scenery.

The page is calm and plainly structured. Its readers are mostly 40 to 70, on a phone, often inside Facebook's in-app browser. After a UX research brief for that audience, Ellie kept the I-95 world and adopted a plainer structure. On 2026-10-15 she asked for more colour, which put the hero on a green guide sign that now stands in a still roadside scene at dusk. A blue services sign for the driver, tried the same day, was dropped: "the blue goes with nothing and the repeated signs feel ugly". Later that day she replaced the amber hero Call button, "obnoxiously bright", with the clickable number itself, and removed the number from the header. The hero sign holds a large headline (its second line, "I'll drive.", in sign yellow), the phone number lettered on the sign as the call itself, a plain "or send a text" link, and the owner's name, in sign yellow, beside a slot for his photo. Then come a plain meet-the-driver block, three numbered steps, plain questions including cost, and a close that opens on the road band with the car driving it, then a big phone number. On phones a small Call pill is never far away. The car is the one moving thing on the page. The road is scenery to watch, not a toy: it takes no touch, the page scrolls straight over it, and nothing on the page asks to be tapped open. Body text is 18px, every call and text control is at least 48px tall (the Send a text button 58px), every word is visible on arrival, and nothing depends on hover.

The world rejects the trucking-company template: no fleet photos, no quote forms, no badges. Type is the system font stack set heavy (700–850), which is Ellie's pitch-site house style. Section headlines are uppercase; the hero headline is mixed case.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One interstate sign carries the colour: the hero is a green guide sign on two posts above a road at dusk. The header sits on night above the scene; then come one long cool-aluminum run (Meet your driver included) and a blue-green night close. No pure black anywhere as a surface.
- One sign, once: the green guide sign with its 3px sign-white face border is the hero, and nothing else on the page is a sign panel.
- One warm colour, amber, reserved at rest for the phone Call pill and the road centre lines. There is no Call button: wherever the page asks for a call, the number itself is the link.
- Shield blue appears only when something is hovered or focused.
- Three photo slots wait for Ed: a wide photo in the desktop hero, a round face beside the owner line on phones, a portrait beside Ed's introduction.
- Only the car in the close moves. The hero's roadside scene, seams, sections and text are still.
- Everything is visible: no accordions, no tabs, no tap-to-reveal.
- The phone number is always written out, and on phones a small Call pill is never far away.
- Heavy system sans with big tap targets, built for older eyes and thumbs.

## Colors

The palette is an interstate: guide-sign green with sign-white and sign-yellow lettering, a dusk sky, cool aluminum, a blue-green night ground, one headlight amber, and a shield blue that appears only on interaction.

### Primary
- **Interstate Guide Green** (sign): the hero guide sign, green inside its sign-white border and again as a 10px rim outside it, like a real guide sign. Also the state-line signs drawn on the canvas, the numbered discs in How it works, the small square bullets in What I do, and Ed's cell number in Meet your driver (its underline at 35%, sign-35). It is also shamrock green. Use it for signage and wayfinding marks, never as a button fill.
- **Sign Edge Green** (sign-deep): the ground of the two hero photo slots (the wide desktop photo and the round phone face), and the 1px outer edge of the hero sign's green rim. It also draws the roof-box straps in the trunk illustration.
- **Sign White** (sign-white): legend colour on the hero sign: the hero headline's first line, the hero's 3px inset border, the hero number (its underline at 50%, sign-white-50), the owner line; also the clover in the favicon, the step numbers, focus rings in the header and the hero, and canvas road-edge lines (80% alpha) and reflector dots (30%). At 60% (sign-white-60) it is the dashed border of the two hero photo slots; at full strength, their icon and caption. It is a raw literal in the source, not a `:root` property.

### Secondary
- **Sign Yellow** (sun): the second lettering colour on the hero sign, added by Ellie on 2026-10-15 ("add some color to the text on the sign"). Used only there: the headline's second line ("I'll drive."), the hero phone icon, and the owner's name. 4.9:1 on sign green. It is lettering, softer than amber, and never a fill, border, underline or interaction colour; amber keeps calling (the pill) and the centre lines.
- **Headlight Amber** (amber): at rest, the fill of the phones-only Call pill, the road canvas centre line (dashed 16/16), the centre dashes of the hero road, the lane line on both seams, and `::selection`. On hover, the underline of the hero number and the hero text link. In the hero scene it is also the warm end of the horizon glow (42%). Text on amber is always ink.

### Tertiary
- **Interstate Shield Blue** (focus): hover borders and underlines on the Send a text button, the close number, the header nav and footer links; `:focus-visible` outlines on night.
- **Deep Shield Blue** (focus-deep): the focus role on aluminum, dark enough for 3:1 against paper, and the hover underline of Ed's number in Meet your driver.
- **Shamrock Leaf** (leaf): the clover in the header lockup only. Not an interface colour.

### Neutral
- **I-95 Night** (night): the page ground (the header sits on it), the top of the hero sky, and the close, where the road band runs. The same value is **Ink** (ink): all text on aluminum (headlines, ledes, steps, services, questions and answers) and text on amber. There is no secondary grey text on light grounds. **Night Clear** (night-0) is night at zero alpha, the top stop of the fade that dissolves the road band into the close.
- **Dusk Sky** (night, dusk-mid, dusk-low): the hero's vertical sky gradient behind the sign: night at the top, dusk-mid at 55%, dusk-low at the horizon. Used nowhere else.
- **Night Shoulder** (night-2): footer ground, one step lighter than the page.
- **Sign-back Aluminum** (paper): the one light ground. Meet your driver, How it works / What I do, Driven not trucked, and Questions sit on one continuous aluminum run.
- **Photo Slot Aluminum** (paper-deep): one step darker than paper, the ground of the portrait slot. Its placeholder dashes are ink at 35% (ink-35).
- **Headlight Haze** (on-dark): text on the green sign (the hero text link) and on night (header nav, close headline and number, footer name and link). At 55% (on-dark-55) it is the Text button's outline; at 40% (on-dark-40) it is the close number's underline. **Dim Haze** (on-dark-soft): secondary text on night (the closing lede, the tagline, the footer lines).
- **White** (white): the Text button's label on hover only.
- **Guardrail** (rule): solid 2px rules framing the question pairs. **Guardrail Dotted** (rule-dotted): 2px dotted rules between services.
- **Road Band** (road-band to road-band-2): the vertical gradient behind the canvas, visible before the first frame paints. Road-band is also the fill of the seam that leads into the close, so the lane line runs straight onto the road.
- **Shadow tints** (shadow-roadside, shadow-pill): the only two shadow colours outside the canvas, both night-tinted: 60% under the hero sign, 55% under the Call pill.

### Named Rules
**The One Headlight Rule.** At rest, amber is used only for the phone Call pill and the road centre lines (the canvas centre dash, the centre dashes of the hero road, and the lane line on the seams). Its one other interface job is a response: the hero number and the hero text link take an amber hover underline, because they sit on the green sign, where shield blue would sink. Every other link hovers in shield blue (deep shield blue on aluminum). Don't use amber at rest for buttons, links, borders, icons or headings. Outside the interface amber appears only as scenery: the warm end of the dusk glow behind the hero's posts, and the cargo in the two car drawings, which is what "packed" looks like at that size.

**The Shield Blue Only Responds Rule.** Interstate-shield blue appears only as a response to the pointer or keyboard: hover borders and underlines, focus outlines (shield blue on night, deep shield blue on aluminum). It is never at rest: never a ground, a fill, or a link colour.

**The Never Black Rule.** Dark grounds are blue-green night (night, night-2), or guide green, never #000. Black appears only inside the canvas; the hero sign's and the Call pill's shadows are night-tinted.

## Typography

**Display Font:** System UI stack (-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, Segoe UI, Roboto, Helvetica Neue, Arial)
**Body Font:** Same stack
**Label/Mono Font:** None. There is one family.

**Character:** One family, set heavy. Weight 850 for headlines and phone numbers, 700–800 for titles, questions, links and buttons, 400 for reading text. It reads like highway lettering. The stack is Ellie's pitch-site house style (no webfont request); Roboto is in it only as the Android fallback.

- **Display** (850, clamp(3rem, 14vw, 5.2rem), 1, -0.02em; **Display Wide** clamp(3rem, 6vw, 4.4rem) from 760px, so it fits the 880px sign): mixed case on the green hero sign: the first line sign-white, the second sign yellow. Only the hero headline ("You fly. / I'll drive."), one phrase per line.

### Named Rules
**The System Stack Rule.** Ellie's house style: the native system sans and nothing else on this site. Hierarchy comes from weight, size, and case.

**The Number Is Written Out Rule.** Wherever the page asks someone to call, the number 732-300-3835 is visible in text and is itself the tap-to-call link: on the hero sign, in Meet your driver, in the close, and in the footer. It is not in the header and not inside a button. The Call pill is the one place it lives only in the accessible label, because the pill must stay small.

## Layout

A single column of full-bleed bands inside a 1120px container with a 20px gutter. The page runs, top to bottom: the header on night; the hero, a roadside scene at dusk with the green guide sign on its posts; a seam onto aluminum; Meet your driver (plain, on aluminum); How it works beside What I do; Driven, not trucked; Questions; a seam onto the road; the close, which opens with the road band and then carries the headline, lede, phone number and buttons; the footer. One roadside sign, one light run, one dark close.

The header sits on night and pads 14px 20px 4px. The hero below it is a still roadside scene: a sky gradient, the guide sign, its posts, and the road. The sign starts 30px below the header (48px at 760px and up). Its face is a sign-green panel with a 3px sign-white border at 16px radius, 34px in from each screen edge on phones (padding 24/18/28), capped at 880px and centred from 760px (padding 40/44/44); the 10px green rim and 1px edge sit outside the border. Inside the face is one left-aligned column, each child capped at 680px, 20px apart: headline, then the call group (6px above, 6px inside: the hero number, then the text link), then the owner line with its round face slot. At 960px and up the face splits into two equal columns 56px apart: copy on the left, the wide photo slot on the right spanning the copy's rows and centred against it; the round face slot steps aside. Under the sign, 11px down to clear the rim, the posts band is 64px tall (96px from 760px): two steel posts (10px wide, 14px from 760px) at 30% and 70% whose feet run 26px (34px from 760px) down into the grass beside the road, a single continuous treeline along its foot (34px tall, 52px from 760px) that shrinks toward the centre and opens a small notch where the road disappears, and a warm horizon glow behind them. Then the ground, 150px (200px from 760px): grass, a road in perspective that starts as a point on the horizon at the centre (the vanishing point) and widens past the screen edges, its edge lines converging on that point, and amber centre dashes that grow toward the viewer. The seam onto aluminum overlaps the foot of the road by its own height (-44px, -64px from 760px).

On phones everything else stacks. At 760px and up:
- the header adds a three-link section nav (Meet your driver / How it works / Questions), 22px apart, with the brand lockup pushed left
- How it works and What I do sit side by side, 1fr / 1fr with a 64px gap
- Driven, not trucked splits 1.1fr / 1fr, copy beside the trunk drawing
- Meet your driver puts the portrait slot in a 260px column beside the text (64px gap, centred) instead of above it
- the Call pill is removed
- the hero sign caps at 880px, the headline steps down to Display Wide, and the posts and road grow
- at 960px, the hero sign face splits into copy and wide photo

Questions cap at 760px wide so answers keep a readable measure on desktop.

Section padding is 44px top / 64px bottom on phones and 56 / 88 at 760px and up. Meet your driver has its own: 28 / 56 on phones and 48 / 72 at 760px and up, with a 30px gap between portrait and text on phones. Blocks that follow another block on the same ground close up to 8px at the top, so the aluminum run reads as one surface. The close has no top padding: the road band sits flush under its seam, full bleed, 190px tall on phones and 260px from 760px, and a 40px gradient at its foot dissolves it into night. The close copy starts 36px below and the section ends 56px down. The footer carries 104px of bottom padding on phones so the Call pill never covers its last line (40px at 760px and up). The default vertical gap inside a section is 22px (lede margin 18px, close number 18px below the lede, buttons 22px below it).

Breakpoints:
- **760px** shows the header nav, switches to the split layouts, hides the Call pill
- **960px** splits the hero sign face into copy and the wide photo slot, and hides the round face slot
- **canvas under 700px wide** narrows the road (60px instead of 78px) and the canvas sign type
- **`(hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`** hides every Send a text control, since `sms:` does nothing on most desktops

### Named Rules
**The Two Buttons Max Rule.** A contact group holds at most two controls: the phone number as a link first, then, on touch devices only, a way to text. The hero pairs the number lettered on the sign with a plain "or send a text" link; the close pairs the big number with a Send a text outline button. Text controls are hidden on mouse devices, where `sms:` does nothing. Don't add a third channel, a duplicate, or a Call button to a group.

**The No Sticky Bar, One Small Pill Rule.** No full-width sticky or fixed bars or banners, ever. A single small labeled Call pill is allowed on phones only: amber, fully rounded, "Call Ed", pinned 16px from the bottom-right corner (plus the safe-area inset). It shows only after the hero call group has scrolled away and hides while the close number is on screen, so it never doubles a visible call. This is a deliberate per-site exception Ellie made on 2026-10-14 for this 40–70 audience; it does not carry to other sites.

**The Nothing Hidden Behind A Tap Rule.** No accordions, tabs, carousels, "read more" or tap-to-reveal. Every question shows its answer in full and every step shows its text. If text is worth having on the page, it is visible on arrival; if it isn't, cut it.

## Elevation & Depth

The page is flat except for the hero sign and the pill. Aluminum and night sections use no shadows: depth comes from the change of ground and from the seams. The hero sign is lifted because a real sign stands off its post: it hangs over its posts and road on a long night-tinted drop. The portrait in Meet your driver currently sits on a hard 14px offset block of sign green (`box-shadow: 14px 14px 0`, no blur). That is not elevation and not part of the shadow vocabulary: it is a carried exception, not a device to reuse. The Call pill is lifted because it floats over the page. Inside the road canvas, the car, trees, houses and state signs cast small offset shadows at 28–35% black. That is the canvas's own top-down lighting, not UI elevation.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Roadside Sign** (`box-shadow: 0 0 0 10px #0a6337, 0 0 0 11px #084d2b, 0 22px 40px -10px rgba(5,15,25,.6)`): the hero sign only. The first two layers are not elevation: they paint the 10px green rim outside the white border and its 1px sign-deep metal edge. The third is a night-tinted drop that lifts the sign off the dusk sky.
- **Pill Float** (`box-shadow: 0 8px 20px -6px rgba(5,20,25,.55)`): the Call pill only, a night-tinted diffuse shadow that separates it from whatever scrolls beneath.

### Named Rules
**The Sign And The Pill Stand Up Rule.** Only the hero sign and the floating Call pill get elevation. Buttons, links, step discs, lists, question pairs, photo slots and sections stay flat.

## Shapes

Corners are soft. The hero sign's face is a 3px sign-white border at 16px on green; its 10px green rim and 1px edge are spread shadows, so they follow the same curve outward. The hero posts are plain square-ended steel. Photo slots are 12px (the wide hero photo), 8px (the portrait), or a full circle (the face). Buttons are 12px. The brand link is 8px, so its focus ring hugs the lockup. Focus rings are 6px. Service bullets are 12px squares at 3px. Step numbers sit in 44px circles. The Call pill is the only fully rounded control (999px), which marks it as a different object from the in-page buttons. Section edges are never straight lines: each change of ground is a wavy edge drawn by a seam.

### Steps (How it works)
Three static numbered steps, never a carousel or a timeline animation.

### Seams (signature)
A still lane line along the top edge of the next ground.
- **Form:** an SVG with a 1200×64 viewBox stretched full width, 44px tall (64px at 760px and up), sitting in flow between bands. The fill path takes the next ground (`--next`): paper over the foot of the hero road, which it overlaps by its own height, road-band above the close. The stroke is a 3px dashed lane line (26 on, 22 off, round caps) in `--lane`.
- **Wave:** `y = 34 + 9·sin(x/190 + φ) + 5·sin(x/83 + 2φ)`, sampled every 20 units, with φ = 1.7 × seam index so no two seams match. Drawn once on load; it never animates.
- **Colour:** amber lane on both seams: the first leaves the hero road for aluminum, the second leaves aluminum for the road.

### Road Canvas (signature)
A top-down night highway band that opens the close: scenery to watch, never a control.
- **Scene:** a winding two-lane road across eight states (New Jersey to Florida, 900px of road each). Ground warms from cool green in Jersey to warmer green in Florida. Shoulder gravel, asphalt, sign-white edge lines at 80%, an amber centre dash 16/16, still reflector dots at 30% sign-white. Trees go pine to oak to palm on the way south. A house sits at each end of the trip. The packed cream car (amber and green cargo) carries an additive headlight beam. A 40px night fade covers its foot.
- **Motion:** the car drives at a steady CRUISE of 110px/s from one house to the other, pauses 2.5s, turns round and drives back. The camera and lane position ease in; nothing else in the scene moves. Direction starts southbound September to February and northbound March to August.
- **Input:** none. No drag, no steering, no hint, no `touch-action` override: the page scrolls normally over the band.
- **Running:** one shared requestAnimationFrame loop (frame time capped at 50ms), paused whenever the band is off screen. Road width 60px when the canvas is under 700px wide, 78px otherwise; devicePixelRatio capped at 2.
- **Reduced motion:** draws one still frame.

**The One Moving Thing Rule.** Only the car in the close moves. The hero roadside scene (sky, sign, posts, treeline, road), seams, text, illustrations and sections never animate, reveal on scroll, or loop. The only other changes on the page are state feedback (colour shifts and a 1px press) in direct response to a touch, hover or keyboard focus, and the Call pill's short fade in and out, which is switched off under reduced motion.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** sign the page like I-95, once: the hero as a green guide sign on two posts above the road, with a 3px sign-white face border at 16px and a 10px green rim outside it.
- **Do** keep amber at rest for the Call pill and the centre lines; the hero number and text link, on the green sign, take an amber hover underline, and every other link hovers in shield blue. Text on amber is always ink.
- **Do** keep sign yellow as lettering on the hero sign only: the headline's second line, the phone icon, the owner's name.
- **Do** write the phone number out wherever the page asks for a call, and make the number itself the link.
- **Do** show shield blue only on hover (gated behind `(hover:hover)`) and focus. Use deep shield blue on aluminum and sign-white focus in the header and the hero.
- **Do** keep each photo slot's shape when Ed's photo lands: 4:3 at 12px for the wide hero photo, a 56px circle for the face, 4:5 at 8px for the portrait, image set to cover.
- **Do** stitch every change of ground with a still seam whose fill is the next ground's colour.
- **Do** keep reading text at 18px or larger, tap targets at 48px or more, the Send a text button at 58px and the Call pill at 56px.
- **Do** show every answer and every step in full, on arrival.
- **Do** keep the car the only moving thing: run it on the shared loop, pause it off screen, and draw a still frame under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Do** write icons as inline SVG symbols that inherit `currentColor`.

### Don't:
- **Don't** add a second moving thing: no rolling seams, scroll reveals, marquees, looping illustrations, or extra traffic in the road.
- **Don't** make the road band interactive or capture touch on it. It is scenery; the page must scroll over it.
- **Don't** hide text behind a tap: no accordions, tabs, carousels or "read more".
- **Don't** add a full-width sticky or fixed bar or banner, ever. The one allowed floating element is the small labeled Call pill on phones.
- **Don't** put more than two contact controls in a group, bring back an amber Call button, or use amber at rest for anything but the Call pill and the centre lines.
- **Don't** use shield blue at rest as a link colour, accent, fill or ground.
- **Don't** add a second sign panel (a blue driver sign was tried and dropped), or animate anything in the hero scene: the sign, posts, treeline and road are a still drawing.
- **Don't** hand the site over with a dashed placeholder still in a photo slot.
- **Don't** use sign yellow off the hero sign, as a fill, border or underline, or in place of amber for calling.
- **Don't** use #000 or near-black as a ground. Night is blue-green.
- **Don't** put a tracked uppercase label above a headline. The page has no tracked uppercase text.
- **Don't** add shadows to buttons, links, step discs, lists, question pairs or sections. Only the hero sign and the Call pill stand up, and don't spread hard offset blocks like the one behind the portrait to other elements.
- **Don't** use stock fleet photos, quote forms, or trust badges.
